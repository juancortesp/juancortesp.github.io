Do tourist flats push up rents in Madrid?

I combined 5 public sources to analyse 100 neighbourhoods and separate the effect of tourist flats from that of location and income. The result: a Power BI dashboard so anyone can explore the data.

By Juan Cortés Paredes · Data Analyst · Updated November 2026

Short answer

Yes, but only slightly. Neighbourhoods with more tourist flats have somewhat higher rents even after accounting for income and distance to the centre, but the effect is at most ~€31/month for a 70 m² flat (2.2% of the average rent). What really sets the price is location and income; the impact is only noticeable in the Centro district.

The question

Are tourist flats to blame for rising rents?

There's a lot of talk about whether tourist flats are to blame for rising rents. But the neighbourhoods with the most Airbnbs also tend to be the most central and in-demand, so simply comparing prices doesn't answer the question.

Do neighbourhoods with more tourist flats have higher rents once income, distance to the centre and population growth are taken into account?

Findings

Results: rent, tourist flats and income by neighbourhood

01

Rent is a squeeze across almost the whole city

  • Average rent for a 70 m² flat: €1,483/month.
  • A 100 m² flat exceeds 30% of household income in 91 out of 100 neighbourhoods.
  • In low-income neighbourhoods rent takes 47% of income, twice as much as in high-income ones (23%).

Rent burden as a share of household income

02

Tourist flats are highly concentrated

  • 43% of listings are in a single district: Centro.
  • Madrid average: 1.8 tourist flats per 100 homes. In Sol: 26.7.
  • The 6 most touristic neighbourhoods are all in the Centro district.

Density of tourist listings by neighbourhood

03

Location and income matter most

The model explains 70% of price differences between neighbourhoods. For a 70 m² flat:

  • −€55/month for every km further from the centre.
  • +€14/month for every extra €1,000 of income per person in the neighbourhood.
  • +€12/month for every point of population growth.

And tourist flats? Their effect is statistically significant (p = 0.033), but small: at most about €31/month for a 70 m² flat, 2.2% of the average rent.

Distance to the centre and income vs price per m²

04

Three neighbourhood profiles

With K-means (elbow method and silhouette score), neighbourhoods group into tourist centre, expensive residential and affordable residential.

The tourist centre has 7 times more tourist flats than expensive residential areas, but almost the same price. Its problem is that its residents earn less.

Each dot is a neighbourhood; colour shows its profile

05

Conclusions

What it means for decision-making

📍

Location and income set the price

Distance to the centre and neighbourhood income explain most of the differences in rent.

🏠

Tourist flats add a little

The effect is real, but in most neighbourhoods it amounts to a few euros a month. Where it really shows is the Centro district.

🎯

Targeted measures work better

Regulating tourist flats would have a real impact in Centro; in the rest of the city, the problem is rent relative to income.

💶

The challenge is affordability

With an average of €1,483/month for 70 m², rent exceeds the 30%-of-income threshold in almost the whole city.
